The question
“Which foundation doesn't oxidise by lunchtime?”
0:00
Hook — the question, word for word
“Which foundation doesn't oxidise by lunchtime?”
Why: the exact words a buyer typed. Matches the query, stops the scroll, anchors the transcript. Never paraphrase it.

THE SENTENCE THAT GETS QUOTED
The answer — immediately, and self-contained
“Match it on your jaw, in daylight — and go slightly cooler than feels right, because most foundations darken a little once they've been on your skin an hour.”
Why: it answers with a method rather than a product, and the method works in any shop. Highly liftable.
0:09
The proof — one mechanism, plainly
“What people call oxidising is mostly the pigment settling into your own oils. The oilier your skin, the bigger the shift.”
Why: one mechanism, and it tells the viewer whether this will happen to them specifically.
0:15
The honest caveat — do not cut this
“A primer buys you time, it doesn't stop it. And if it's going properly orange on you, that's the shade — no formula fixes the wrong shade.”
Why: kills the two things the category usually upsells. Doing that is what makes the recommendation credible.
0:21
The product — last, as evidence
“This is the one that stays put on me — same shade at six as it was at eight.”
Why: an observation with a timeframe attached, which reads as evidence rather than a claim.
